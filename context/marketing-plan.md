## 1. Executive Summary

Rya Rightsizing is a senior rightsizing and move management service owned and operated by Lisa Fisher, offering tailored, compassionate support to keep senior parents in control of every decision. Services include a complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual (45 minutes [NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: confirm length]), an $80 needs assessment [NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: confirm it is offered and public], and hourly support from $80/hour per specialist, booked in three-hour blocks of time or more.

**Target market**

West Vancouver was chosen as the target market for this cycle, based on the following:
- High senior population: 28.5% of residents are aged 65+ (vs. 19.0% nationally) (Statistics Canada, 2022). [verify citation: the original cited Government of Canada, n.d., which is the Squamish profile.]
- Median household income of $104,000 (vs. $90,000 for Metro Vancouver) [SOURCE NEEDED] and BC's highest rate of mortgage-free homeownership (56%) (North Shore News, n.d.-a). Internal comparison only; do not use in public copy.
- Active senior community: the Seniors' Activity Centre logged 4,631 active memberships at year-end 2025 (District of West Vancouver Seniors' Activity Centre, n.d., unaudited).

**Campaign strategy and objectives**

Over 12 weeks with a $500 CAD per month budget, the campaign aims to generate 14 qualified inquiries, 6 consultations and 2 paid clients.

The plan relies on three strategies:
- **S1 Community Connection:** networking through local residences, the Seniors' Activity Centre, realtors and social workers.
- **S2 Educational Seminars:** complimentary seminars to build trust. [NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: confirm seminars are a Rya offer.]
- **S3 Digital Visibility:** optimizing the Google Business Profile, local content, repeated AI search testing, and a small geo-targeted ad test.

**Execution and risks**

The campaign rolls out as four 3-week storylines (Siblings at the Table, Who Gets the Good China?, The Photo Album Hour and Measure First), backed by 48 pre-built assets that need about 1 hour a week of approval. [NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: Lisa to confirm the approval time.] Key risks are Lisa's operational capacity (traveling from Squamish, which requires batched local workdays) and early job economics relative to the cost of winning a client.

## 4. Strategies and positioning

**Positioning and differentiation.** For adult children assisting senior parents with moving out of a family home, Rya Rightsizing provides one-on-one, tailored move management. Unlike a moving or junk-removal company, Rya treats a lifetime of belongings as a story to honour, not a load to clear. Rya offers a single, empathetic guide from the first meeting to settling into the new place. The service blends emotional care with practical logistics, ensuring senior parents remain fully in control of every decision.

**Core growth strategies**
- **S1 Community Connection:** build trust directly through high-touch local venues such as the Seniors' Activity Centre, senior residences and other key referral partners, rather than relying strictly on advertising.
- **S2 Educational Seminars:** host pressure-free workshops ("teach first, then invite") to offer practical value, using the complimentary consultation as a natural, low-stress next step.
- **S3 Digital Visibility:** establish a complete Google Business Profile targeted at West Vancouver and consistently publish local, FAQ-driven content to make Rya easy to find and verify online.

**Storylines.** The campaign moves families through four stages over 12 weeks. Each storyline builds on the one before it. The first introduces the problem, the second builds trust, the third brings the emotional side into focus, and the fourth makes the move easy to take on. See Appendix A.

Every stage includes a call to action, so families are invited to take the next step each time.

## 6. 12-week campaign roadmap

The 12 weeks run as four coordinated three-week campaigns, each built around one family conversation. Together they move adult children and their parents from Introduction (weeks 1 to 4) to Build trust (weeks 5 to 8) to Invite action (weeks 9 to 12), while referral partners receive the same content as printed resources they can pass on. Every campaign offers a low-pressure step first (a complimentary guide or card) and a complimentary consultation second, to reduce friction and hesitation to call.

### How the campaigns contribute to the objectives
Weeks 1 to 4 build awareness, measured by reach, saves, guide downloads and email sign-ups. Weeks 5 to 8 build trust through referral partners, talks and seminars, because trust through people is the main route to clients. Weeks 9 to 12 convert, with the complimentary consultation as the lead call to action. S1 shows up in the calendar as partner meetings and talks. Together they work toward the primary objective of 14 qualified inquiries, 6 consultations and 2 new paid clients, alongside 8 to 10 partner meetings and at least 3 talks. KPIs are reviewed every four weeks, and the AI search test is repeated in weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12.

## Appendix A. Storylines

**Storyline 1, weeks 1 to 3: Siblings at the Table (C1).** Most families start with disagreement. Adult children often want different things for a parent, and the process stalls before it begins. This storyline names that situation and offers a way forward: one plan the whole family can accept.
- What families hear: their differences are normal, and a planned process can help them agree.
- What Rya offers: a single point of contact who helps the family stay organized.
- The step: book a complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual.

**Storyline 2, weeks 4 to 6: Who Gets the Good China? (C2).** Once the family is talking, the question becomes who decides. This storyline puts the parents at the center. The parent chooses who receives meaningful belongings, so the family is not making those choices for them.
- What families hear: the parent's voice matters, and the decisions are still theirs.
- What Rya offers: a process that keeps the parent in control.
- The step: take the conversation card and start the discussion at home.

**Storyline 3, weeks 7 to 9: The Photo Album Hour (C3).** With the decisions in place, the sorting begins, and that is where the emotion often surfaces. This storyline recognizes that belongings carry memories, and that sorting can be a chance to hear them. A seminar in week 9 is the main invitation.
- What families hear: sorting is more than clearing a home. It can be a time to hear family stories.
- What Rya offers: support that respects the meaning behind each item.
- The step: join the session.

**Storyline 4, weeks 10 to 12: Measure First (C4).** The final storyline shows families that the planning is handled for them. Lisa measures the new place, so the family does not have to prepare anything in advance. The measurements then shape what comes along, which makes the decision more concrete.
